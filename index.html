I study how humans and artificial systems see the world—and how confidently we can compare the representations they build.

At Columbia’s Visual Inference Lab, I work with Nikolaus Kriegeskorte on the measures used to compare neural and model representations. I’m interested in what these measures preserve, what they ignore, and where apparently strong brain–model alignment can be misleading.

Before Columbia, I completed dual bachelor’s degrees in Statistics & Data Science and Psychological & Brain Sciences at UC Santa Barbara. In the Vision & Image Understanding Lab, I studied visual search, brain–model alignment, and the shortcuts neural networks learn from structured scenes.

Currently in New York City · always happy to talk about vision, representation, and computational neuroscience.
